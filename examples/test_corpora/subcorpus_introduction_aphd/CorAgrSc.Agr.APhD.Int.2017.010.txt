Seagrass meadows seagrass or vegetated soft bottom are ecosystems formed by																																																																																																													
one or more angiosperm species that occur in the shallows coastal zone Coles et al 2011 Short et al 2007 These plants tend to form an extensive vegetated area on subaqueous soils where clear waters allow the penetration of light through the water column Coles et al 2011 Generally the plants comprising the meadow grow in areas protected from the action of the waves Phillips et al 1988 to a maximum depth of 91 m Duarte 1991 However most of the meadows are found in shallowerr areas with depth less than 10 m Grech et al																																																																																																													
2012																																																																																																													
This ecosystem is widely distributed around the globe throughout temperate and																																																																																													
tropical coastal Short et al 2007 and in more than 120 countries and territories except in Antartica Spalding et al 2003 2010 and covers an extimated area between 300000 to 600000 km2 Duarte et al 2005 Fourqurean et al 2012																																																																																																													
Seagrasses are the only underwater marine plants having roots and rhizomes Short et al 2007 These species have flowers for sexual reproduction resulting in the formation viviparous seed Olsen et al 2016 however most seagrasses reproduction is based on vegetative propagation Short et al 2007 Additionally seagrasses have evolved to a complete development in subaquatic environments withstanding salinity stress growing and developing fully submerged pollinated by water and a root system that fixes the plants to soil against the actions of the tide and the waves Phillips et al 1988																																																																																																													
This ecosystem is one of  the richest and most important ecosystems on Earth performing a series of ecological functions and supporting many species at all trophic levels Orth et al 2006 Short et al 2011 In fact seagrasses correspond to one of the most productive ecosystems on the planet Duarte et al 2010 2005 Duarte and Chiscano 1999 Olsen et al 2016 providing nutrients mainly N and P and organic carbon to other parts of the ocean including deeper regions Short et al 2011 The high primary productivity is an essential function of these ecosystem Phillips et al 1988 characterizing it as the base of many marine food webs Coles et al 2011																																																																																																													
In  addition  seagrass  meadows  are  components  of  a  complex  ecosystem  in  the																																																																																																													
coastal  zone  that  contribute  to  the  maintenance  of  adjacent  coastal  ecosystems  eg																																																																																																													
mangroves salt marshes and coral reefs Short et al 2007 2006 Seagrass habitats act as																																																																																																													
nurseries for many endangered species eg dugongs manatees turtles Short et al 2007 and those of high commercial value eg shrimps lobsters and shellfish Heck Jr et al 2003 Other ecological functions include the maintenance of genetic variability and potential biochemical exploration and stabilization of the coastal zone by reducing the energy of waves and currents Bos et al 2007 Short et al 2007 Recently seagrasses and other coastal wetlands have been recognized as key elements for reducing carbon concentrations in the atmosphere and minimizing the effects of global warming Chmura 2013 Chmura et al 2003 Grimsditch et al 2013 Mcleod et al 2011 which led to the creation of the term Blue Carbon sinks referring to the C stocks in coastal areas Nellemann et al 2009																																																																																																													
Given the important role that seagrass meadows play these ecosystems have the highest economic value Costanza et al 1997 equivalent to US 28000 ha1 yr1 2010 values Constanza et al 2014 The economic value of its services is even greater when considering the fisheries production approximately U1000 ha1 yr1 Tuya et al 2014 and carbon sequestration Pendleton et al 2012 In more detailed local studies the value of some seagrass areas can reach almost US 2 million ha1  yr1 172  m2  yr1 Vassallo et al																																																																																																													
2013																																																																																																													
Despite its importance these ecosystems have been impacted by the development																																																																																																													
of cities and industries in coastal areas and unsustainable fishing practices that have caused a rapid decline of seagrass meadows across the world Gladstone and Courtenay 2014 Nordlund and Gullström 2013 Globally about 30 of seagrass meadows have been lost in the last 50 years Mckenzie et al 2012 Waycott et al 2009 Thus the knowledge and monitoring of these areas would be able to avoid irreparable loss or facilitate the use of corrective practices for ecosystem restoration Short et al 2006 In this sense pedological studies of seagrass meadows soils may provide useful tools for the management and protection of these ecosystems since it may help to understand the interrelationship between soils and seagrasses
